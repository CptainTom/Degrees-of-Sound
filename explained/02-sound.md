# 02 – The Sound: how you hear the temperature

## The idea
You stand on a spot on the globe. Press **S** and that spot plays a **little tune**. Move, or change the year, and the tune changes with the temperature there.

Nothing plays until you ask. **The listener explores.**

## What each sound means
| You hear… | It means… |
|---|---|
| **How high the tune is** | **The temperature.** Higher = warmer than normal, lower = colder. Normal is always the same note (middle C). |
| **How fast the tune is** | **Also the temperature.** 72 beats per minute at 2 °C colder, up to 120 at 2 °C warmer. Two clues for one meaning makes it easier to hear. |
| **The instrument** | **Warmer or colder.** Warm = a soft electric-piano bell, cold = a wooden marimba. |
| **How busy it is** | **How unusual.** Far from normal, the tune plays twice as many notes. |
| **The low hum underneath** | **The whole planet's average** for that year. It rises as the world warms. |
| **Enter** | **The whole line** from the North Pole to the South Pole through your spot, like a harp. The first notes are the Arctic, and your spot is the loudest. |

## Why it sounds musical, not like noise
- Every note comes from a **pentatonic scale** (the five-note scale in a lot of folk music), so notes always sound good together and the data can never play a "wrong" chord.
- The tune has a soft bass, a quiet heartbeat kick and a shaker, played in a small "room" (reverb and echo).
- The hum is tuned to the same scale.
- A **limiter** keeps the volume safe.

## Sounds that aren't temperature
Short, soft sounds in the same "room", clearly different from the tune:
- **a two-note chime**: you crossed the equator
- **a low soft thud**: you reached an edge (a pole, or the first or last year)
- **a soft breath**: no measurement here, so silence is never confused with "normal"
- **gentle menu sounds** when menus open, close or move

## Who made what
The audio team designed the tune (tempo, arpeggio, bass, drums), the instruments and the room sound. We kept their rule: **hotter is always higher and faster, never the other way round.**

## How we test it
**Blind test:** a teammate who didn't build the sound closes their eyes, hears the same place in two years at least 30 years apart, and says which was warmer. It has to be right **8 times out of 10**.
