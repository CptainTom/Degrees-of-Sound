# 03 – Accessibility: built for people who can't see the screen

Blind and low-vision people are our main audience, so **Listening mode is the default**. Many ideas come from accessible video games, navigation apps for blind people, and Apple's Audio Graphs.

## Two modes, chosen on the start screen
| | Listening mode (default) | Visual mode |
|---|---|---|
| For | Blind and low-vision people | Sighted visitors |
| Narrator | On: explains everything as you go | Off (screen readers still get every message) |
| Controls | Keyboard only, like a game console | Mouse and keyboard |

We call it "Listening mode", not "blind mode": it describes the experience, not the person, and anyone can enjoy it.

## The narrator
- An English voice describes where you are in plain words: *"Turkey: 2 degrees or more warmer than normal."*
- **Only the newest sentence is spoken.** Holding a key never builds a queue of old sentences.
- The music goes quiet while the narrator speaks, then the new spot's tune starts.
- **One voice at a time:** if you use your own screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver), press **V** to turn our narrator off, and the screen reader gets everything instead.
- Optional **subtitles** show what's being said, for sighted helpers.

## Game-style menus
- **M** opens the Menu and **H** opens Help, both shown as a list like a console game. **↑↓** move (each item is read aloud), **←→** change a setting, **Enter** chooses, **Esc** closes.
- Soft sounds confirm every move, so you know the key worked even before the voice speaks.
- **Help explains every key aloud,** and Enter on an item **tries it right away**. Help is always one key or one button away.
- The **start screen** works the same way: ↑↓ to choose a mode, with each option read aloud, and Enter to start.

## Ways to explore by ear
| Key | Feature | Inspired by |
|---|---|---|
| **S / Space** | Start or stop the music | Media players |
| **T** | **History of this spot**: 1881 → 2023 as a 10-second melody, then a spoken trend | Apple Audio Graphs |
| **C** | **Compare** 1951 with today: *"2023 is 1.5 degrees warmer than 1951"* | — |
| **R / Shift+R** | **Heat radar**: a sound from the *direction* of the warmest (or coldest) place nearby; **G** goes there | Audio games |
| **N** | **Nearby places**, with directions | Soundscape, BlindSquare |
| **W** | **Where am I?** | Accessible games |
| **L** | **Sound tour**: every sound explained, one by one | Game tutorials |
| **/** | **Search** for a country, region or ocean; suggestions are read as you move through them | — |

## Low vision
- **Readable font:** switches the pixel font to a plain one.
- **Bigger text** setting.
- High-contrast colours, clear digits, and a visible focus glow.
- **Reduced motion** is respected: the globe jumps instead of spinning.

## Control and comfort
- **S** stops the music at any time, and **Esc** stops the narrator and any tour. Continuous sound must always be easy to stop (WCAG, the web accessibility standard).
- Volume, voice speed, rhythm and background hum are all adjustable, and remembered for next time.
- **D** downloads this spot's data as a spreadsheet, for people who prefer exact numbers.

## What we found by listening
- **"Coldest nearby" from Greece** points to the North Atlantic south of Iceland, the known "cold blob" where the ocean has warmed least.
- **Greenland in 1930 vs. 2023** is only 0.2 °C apart: Greenland had a known warm period in the 1930s.
